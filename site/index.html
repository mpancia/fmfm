FORMALFRED  ·  M-VAVE FM-1  ·  firmware {{version}}

A generative music box for the M-VAVE FM-1, written in Lean, with theorems about what it plays.

The FM-1 is a pocket keyboard with a 240-pixel screen, seven knobs and a speaker. This firmware gives it four lanes. Each divides the clock into a rhythm (Euclidean patterns, cellular automata, ratchets) and picks notes from a scale (random walks, Markov chains, grammars, Tonnetz chord walks). The voices are all 48 Braids shapes, a four-operator FM voice and an 808, each through a filter, with a reverb and a ping-pong delay on sends. 142 patches to start from, 17 of them demo songs. Alfred bows at boot and stands in the corner when you stop the clock.

The lanes, the voices' arithmetic, the effects, the mix and the patch format are Lean 4, compiled to C and then to the chip. {{theorems}} theorems about that code go through Lean's kernel on every build. What is trusted (the screen, the drivers, the loops that call the Lean, Braids' tables, Lean's C backend, JieLi's clang and the runtime) is listed in the README.

What it does

Rhythm

A lane's bar is Euclidean by default: 1 to 32 steps, hits spread as evenly as possible, at any of eight clock divisions. Or a rotated bar with accents, a cellular automaton, ratchets. A lane's position in its bar depends only on the tick count, so four lanes at four speeds stay in phase (run_clock_never_drifts), and at play chance 100 a bar plays exactly the hits you set (run_bar_plays_every_hit); below 100 it never plays a rest (lane_note_only_on_hit).

Notes

Eleven note sources, among them a random draw, walks, a mutating loop, a Turing register, a Markov chain that learns from the keys, and a grammar. Chord progressions are written as degrees; a lane can follow the chord, its seventh, or the notes outside the key. The last three progressions walk the Tonnetz by P, L and R. The note that comes out is always in the scale (lane_note_in_scale).

Sound

255 sounds: Braids' analog, digital, physical, percussion, vowel, wavetable and noise engines, eight FM shapes, ten 808 drums, each with an envelope and a filter. Every lane has an LFO. The keys have an arpeggiator, a scale or chord quantizer, and a page of punch-in effects. The reverb and the delay are sends.

What is proved

{{theorems}} theorems on the three standard axioms, checked by Lean's kernel and by lake comparator, and each run on concrete inputs too.

AreaTheoremsWhat is promised

Every theorem is in docs/PROOFS.md with its statement, why it matters and the definitions it depends on. The README lists what is trusted: the screen, the drivers, the accessors that read Braids' tables, and the C loops that call the Lean. The proofs are about the Lean the chip's code is compiled from (Lean's C backend and JieLi's clang are trusted); the engine arithmetic is also checked sample for sample against the original Braids.

Before you flash

  • Put the unit on the official V15 firmware first, and take a full-flash backup. Steps in docs/FM1.md.
  • The installer works over USB from Chrome or Edge and keeps the update path and the OCT- rescue.
  • Tested on one FM-1, installed over the stock firmware and over itself. Use at your own risk.
