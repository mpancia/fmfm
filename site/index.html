FORMALFRED  ·  M-VAVE FM-1  ·  firmware {{version}}

A sequencer that plays by itself, for the M-VAVE FM-1, with the things it promises proved in Lean.

The FM-1 is a pocket keyboard with a 240-pixel screen, seven encoders and a speaker, and this firmware replaces what it came with by four lanes that each divide the clock into a rhythm and then choose, note by note, something to play from a scale, so that a bar of sixteenths in C minor and a slower bar on the same chord can sit next to each other for an hour without either one falling behind. The voice is Braids, the Mutable Instruments oscillator, every one of its 48 shapes, with a reverb and a ping-pong delay hanging off a send. There are 128 patches to start from. Alfred the butler bows when the unit boots and goes to stand in the corner of the screen whenever you stop the clock.

Most of what runs is Lean 4: the lanes, the arithmetic of every Braids engine, the two effects, the mix and the patch format are compiled from Lean to C and then to the chip, and {{theorems}} theorems about that code go through Lean's kernel on every build. What is left in C (the screen, the drivers, the loops that call the Lean) the README lists as trusted rather than proved, and nobody has yet heard any of it on a physical FM-1.

What it does

Rhythm

A lane's bar is Euclidean by default, 1 to 32 steps with the hits spread as evenly as they can be, at any of eight clock divisions; or it can be a rotated bar with accents, a one-dimensional cellular automaton, or ratchets. Since where a lane stands in its bar is a function of the tick count alone, four lanes at four speeds come back into line on the bar whatever their notes did in between (run_clock_never_drifts).

Notes

Eleven ways to choose the next note, from a plain random draw through walks, bounces and a mutating loop to a Turing machine, a Markov chain and a small grammar, all of them kept inside a range and a scale. A chord progression can move the whole scale a few semitones or hold a lane to the triad. Whichever you pick, the note that comes out is in the scale (lane_note_in_scale), and that one is a theorem.

Sound

199 sounds, drawn from Braids' analog, digital, physical, percussion, vowel, wavetable and noise engines, each with an envelope and a timbre knob. Every lane has an LFO. The keys have an arpeggiator. Both effects are sends, so a lane can be dry while another swims.

The panel

Four buttons pick a group of pages; the same button again moves to the next page. The four knobs set the four values on screen.

What is proved

{{theorems}} theorems, on the three standard axioms and nothing else, checked by Lean's kernel and replayed by five independent checkers under lake comparator.

AreaTheoremsWhat is promised

Every theorem is listed in docs/PROOFS.md with its statement and a sentence on why anyone should care, and the README says which parts are trusted instead: the screen, the drivers, the one-line accessors that read Braids' tables, and the C loops that call the Lean. The proofs are about the code the chip runs, generated from the same Lean, with the arithmetic of every engine checked sample for sample against the original Braids. They are not about the hardware, which none of this has touched.

Before you flash

  • Put the unit on the official V15 firmware first, and take a full-flash backup. Steps in docs/FM1.md.
  • The installer works over USB from Chrome or Edge and keeps the update path and the OCT- rescue.
  • Nothing here has been run on a physical FM-1. Use at your own risk.
