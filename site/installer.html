A sequencer that plays by itself, with proofs.

Four lanes of Euclidean rhythm, each choosing its own notes, every one of the 48 Braids shapes, chord progressions, and a butler who bows. The sequencer and the voices are written in Lean 4, and what they promise is proved there.

Install the latest release · Try it in your browser · Source and proofs

  • Four lanes

    Each one divides the clock into a Euclidean rhythm and then picks its notes by one of eleven algorithms, anything from a random draw to a Turing machine or a small grammar, inside a range and a scale you set.

  • Every Braids shape

    The 48 oscillator shapes of Mutable Instruments' Braids are all here, arranged into 199 sounds and 128 patches that a browser sorts by engine or by what kind of sound they are.

  • Theorems about the music

    That every note lands in its scale, that a bar has exactly the hits you asked for, that stopping the clock leaves nothing sounding: Lean's kernel checks these against the firmware's own code, not a model of it.

2Connect

Plug the FM-1 in with a USB data cable and close other apps that use its MIDI port. Connect asks the browser for MIDI access with SysEx, then reads the device identity. No firmware data is sent in this step.

3Install

Install opens once the package is valid and the connected device matches it. The running firmware stages the update loader first, then the loader writes the whole image and the FM-1 restarts on its own. The page reports success only when the device comes back with the package identity.

Waiting for a package and a device.

During the write

  • Keep the FM-1 connected and powered, and keep this page open until it reports the installed identity.
  • The device restarts twice: into the update loader, then into the new firmware.
  • If the connection drops, press Connect and then Install again: a device left in update mode resumes the write.
  • If the FM-1 no longer starts, recovery needs FM-1-transporter; this page cannot recover every failure.
