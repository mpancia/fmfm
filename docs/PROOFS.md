## Analog

**`analog_this_blep_range`**: The correction a step edge adds to its own sample is between 0 and 16383.

Why it matters: the band-limiting correction cannot push a sample out of range.

Spec surface: `Analog.thisBlep`

**`analog_next_blep_range`**: The correction a step edge leaves for the next sample is between -16383 and 0.

Why it matters: the band-limiting correction cannot push a sample out of range.

Spec surface: `Analog.nextBlep`

**`analog_triangle_half_range`**: Each half of a triangle step lies between -16384 and 16383.

Why it matters: the two halves add without leaving the 16-bit range.

Spec surface: `Analog.triangleHalf`

**`analog_triangle_never_wraps`**: The triangle's sample is exactly the sum of its two halves; nothing wraps.

Why it matters: the triangle cannot click from an overflow at any pitch or phase.

Spec surface: `Analog.triangle`, `Analog.triangleHalf`

**`analog_sine_between_entries`**: The sine at any phase lies between the two table entries it blends.

Why it matters: the sine, and everything FM-modulated through it, stays within the table's range, so the cut to 16 bits
in `Analog.sineAt` never changes a value.

Spec surface: `Analog.sineAt`, `Analog.sineTable`, `Analog.tableIndex`, `tableIndex`

**`analog_phase_exact`**: An oscillator's phase after `n` samples is exactly `phase + n * inc`, wrapped at 2^32.

Why it matters: the frequency is exactly `inc / 2^32` of the sample rate with no rounding error building up, so a long
note does not drift.

Spec surface: `Analog.freeRun`

## AnalogSetup

**`analog_setup_pulse_width_in_range`**: For a non-negative parameter (Braids' parameters are 0 to 32767), the square's
pulse turns on between 768/32768 and 32768/32768 of the cycle and the variable saw's second slope starts between
1024/32768 and 32767/32768 of it.

Why it matters: the sample code places its edge correction by dividing the distance to the edge by the phase step; an
edge at phase 0 would be corrected twice and one past the cycle never reached.

Spec surface: `AnalogSetup.squarePulseWidth`, `AnalogSetup.variableSawPulseWidth`, `parameter`, `squarePulseWidth`,
`variableSawPulseWidth`

**`analog_setup_buzz_zone_in_table`**: The buzz reads two of the 15 comb tables, both inside the set, for any pitch and
parameter.

Why it matters: the comb tables are C data read without a check; the clamp to the last zone keeps a high or unclamped
pitch from reading past them.

Spec surface: `AnalogSetup.buzzZone`, `AnalogSetup.buzzZone2`, `buzzZone`, `buzzZone2`, `parameter`, `pitch`

## Arp

**`arp_note_is_held`**: Every note the arpeggiator starts is a held note raised by fewer than `octaves` octaves.

Why it matters: the arpeggiator never adds a note of its own, in any mode.

Spec surface: `Arp.Valid`, `Arp.tick`, `Punch.held`, `held`

**`arp_make_valid`**: `Arp.make` gives a valid arpeggiator for any `div`.

Why it matters: the arpeggiator starts in a state the rest of the firmware can rely on.

Spec surface: `Arp.Valid`, `Arp.make`

**`arp_set_valid`**: `Arp.set` keeps a valid arpeggiator valid for any settings.

Why it matters: turning the ARP knobs mid-phrase cannot push a timer or counter out of range.

Spec surface: `Arp.Valid`, `Arp.set`

**`arp_keys_valid`**: Pressing, releasing or clearing keys keeps a valid arpeggiator valid.

Why it matters: the held set stays playable and bounded however many keys are pressed.

Spec surface: `Arp.Valid`, `Arp.clear`, `Arp.press`, `Arp.release`

**`arp_tick_valid`**: A tick keeps a valid arpeggiator valid.

Why it matters: counters stay in range and the sounding note and its timer agree for any number of ticks.

Spec surface: `Arp.Valid`, `Arp.tick`

**`arp_every_note_on_has_a_note_off`**: After any sequence of ticks, keys and setting changes from a fresh arpeggiator,
note-ons equal note-offs plus the one note still sounding, which `silence` stops.

Why it matters: releasing the keys or stopping never leaves an arpeggio note hanging.

Spec surface: `Arp.Op`, `Arp.noteOffs`, `Arp.noteOns`, `Arp.run`, `Arp.trace`

**`arp_up_plays_pattern`**: UP with `div = 1` from position 0 plays the pattern through in order: as many ticks as the
pattern has notes start exactly the pattern.

Why it matters: UP plays each held note once, in order, per lap; the position returns to 0 so later laps read the same.

Spec surface: `Arp.pattern`, `Arp.played`, `Punch.held`, `held`

## Blend

**`blend_eight_between`**: Blending two 16-bit entries by an 8-bit fraction (`Interpolate88`, `Analog.blend88`) lands
between them.

Why it matters: a wave shaper's output stays within its table's range without relying on the 16-bit cut.

Spec surface: `Analog.blend88`

**`blend_sixteen_between`**: Blending two 16-bit values by a 16-bit fraction (`Interpolate824`) lands between them, for
any two values. The product inside can wrap in 32 bits (Braids' C++ does so for steep comb and wave tables), but the
wrap is a multiple of 2^16 and the cut to 16 bits cancels it exactly.

Why it matters: every table read and wave crossfade uses this blend; the overflow in the original is proved harmless.

## BraidsData

**`braids_data_increments_rising`**: The phase step table rises from one entry to the next for every index the code
reads (below 96: `pitch_table_reads_inside`); the entry after the last is the copy's tail, so it is excluded.

Why it matters: `pitch_increment_bounded` blends two neighbouring entries and needs the second to be the larger; this
checks it against the data Braids ships.

Spec surface: `Pitch.incrementTable`, `incrementTable`

**`braids_data_increments_close`**: Two neighbouring phase step entries differ by less than 2^27 for every index the
code reads (below 96).

Why it matters: the blend multiplies that difference by a 4-bit fraction in signed 32-bit arithmetic, and 2^27 * 15 is
the most that fits.

Spec surface: `Pitch.incrementTable`, `incrementTable`

**`braids_data_increments_top`**: No phase step entry passes 2588618730, the table's last, at any index.

Why it matters: the setup shifts the entry down at least one octave, and this top puts the result below half a cycle
(`pitch_increment_bounded`).

Spec surface: `Pitch.incrementTable`, `incrementTable`

**`braids_data_cutoff_rising`**: The filter cutoff table rises from one entry to the next for every index a phase reads
(below 256: `tables_phase_reads_inside_257`).

Why it matters: `filters_setup_cutoff_in_range` blends two neighbouring entries and needs the second to be the larger;
this checks it against the data Braids ships.

Spec surface: `Percussion.cutoffTable`

**`braids_data_cutoff_top`**: No filter cutoff entry passes 25078, the table's last, at any index.

Why it matters: the swarm's high-pass coefficient is a blend of two entries, so this is its range, which the filters'
sample theorems take.

Spec surface: `Percussion.cutoffTable`

**`braids_data_rate_top`**: No grain envelope rate entry passes 32768, the table's last, at any index.

Why it matters: `noise_setup_grain_rate_bounded` shifts the entry up three places and needs the result below 2^18 to
keep the grain's envelope reads inside their table.

Spec surface: `NoiseSetup.rateTable`, `rateTable`

**`braids_data_vowel_amplitudes_are_nibbles`**: Every vowel formant amplitude is a nibble (at most 15), for any vowel
and formant.

Why it matters: the formant tables are indexed by the phase's top nibble or-ed with the amplitude
(`tables_formant_reads_inside_256`); an amplitude above 15 would read past them.

Spec surface: `VowelsSetup.vowelAmplitudeAt`, `vowelAmplitudeAt`

**`braids_data_wavetable_steps_at_most_16`**: Every wavetable definition has at most 16 steps, for any table index.

Why it matters: `wavetables_setup_table_in_table` multiplies the parameter by the step count and needs the pointer's
step and the one after it inside the 17 steps of a definition; Braids ships 4, 8 or 16.

Spec surface: `WavetablesSetup.tableSteps`, `tableSteps`

## Clock

**`clock_advance_never_wraps`**: The clock accumulator never wraps: for a stall of up to 20 s, tempo up to 240 and a
tempo factor up to 200 percent, starting from any residue below the longest swung tick, the 32-bit sum is the exact sum.

Why it matters: after a long main-loop stall (patch load, save, redraw) the clock makes up the missed time instead of
jumping the song.

Spec surface: `Clock.advance`

**`clock_division_is_the_loop`**: `ticksDue` and `remainder` describe the firmware's `while (acc >= len) { acc -= len;
tick(); }` loop: ticks due times the length plus the remainder is the accumulator, and the remainder is below one tick.

Why it matters: every fact about `ticksDue` and `remainder` is a fact about the loop in `dinghy.c`.

Spec surface: `Clock.remainder`, `Clock.ticksDue`

**`clock_half_time_is_half`**: Over the same milliseconds from the same residue, HALF fires half the ticks of straight
time to within one tick of rounding: `2 * half ≤ straight + 2` and `straight ≤ 2 * half + 1` (straight ticks of 10000,
swing 0).

Why it matters: a bar under HALF is two bars long to the tick, so lanes, swing and chord changes stay in step.

Spec surface: `Clock.advance`, `Clock.ticksDue`

**`clock_double_time_is_double`**: Over the same milliseconds from the same residue, DOUBLE fires twice the ticks of
straight time to within one tick of rounding: `double ≤ 2 * straight + 1` and `2 * straight ≤ double + 2` (straight
ticks of 10000, swing 0).

Why it matters: a bar under DOUBLE is half a bar long to the tick, so lanes, swing and chord changes stay in step.

Spec surface: `Clock.advance`, `Clock.ticksDue`

## Delay

**`delay_gains_in_range`**: For every feedback setting the gain is between 0 and 0.95 (31129 in Q15), and for every tone
setting the low-pass amount is between 0.2 and 1.0.

Why it matters: each echo is quieter than the one before at every knob position.

Spec surface: `Delay.feedbackGain`, `Delay.toneGain`

**`delay_echo_shrinks`**: With nothing new on the send, what goes back into the line is at most 95% of the echo plus one
count of rounding, for every feedback setting (the most negative number excluded).

Why it matters: each pass round the line loses at least a twentieth, so the echoes decay at any setting.

Spec surface: `Delay.input`

**`delay_samples_in_line`**: The delay in samples is between 1 and 20479, so the echo is read from inside the line,
never from the sample being written (offset 0) and never from a wrapped position.

Why it matters: no TIME or TEMPO setting makes a line read where it writes, a feedback path with no delay.

Spec surface: `Delay.samples`

**`delay_position_in_line`**: A write position and an offset inside a line give a read position inside it, and the next
write position is inside it.

Why it matters: with `delay_samples_in_line`, every read and write of the two lines is in bounds; the lines are not a
power of two, so the wrap is by subtraction, not a mask.

Spec surface: `Delay.nextWrite`, `Delay.pos`

**`delay_notes_exact`**: At 44.1 kHz the note values the lines are long enough for are exact, `60 * 44100 * numerator /
(bpm * denominator)` samples: 1/32 and 1/16 at every tempo from 40 to 240, everything up to a dotted eighth from 97 bpm,
every note value from 130 bpm. (Below those tempos a longer note is held at 20479 samples, 464 ms.)

Why it matters: the echo lands on the beat the setting names at the tempos it can be played at.

Spec surface: `Delay.denominator`, `Delay.numerator`, `Delay.samples`

**`delay_store_saturates`**: A signal for the line saturates at the 16-bit ends instead of wrapping: from 32767 up it
stores 32767, from -32768 down it stores -32768, and in between it stores the signal.

Why it matters: a send louder than full scale clips instead of flipping sign into a click that echoes.

Spec surface: `Delay.store`

**`delay_echoes_die_away`**: An echo going round the undriven line `n` times is at most its starting size times
`(19/20)^n` plus 20 (the roundings of the hops, one each, kept below 20 by the same ratio), for every feedback setting;
after enough hops it is within 20 counts of silence (a count is 1/32768).

Why it matters: the tail ends at every setting; rounding cannot accumulate into a hum. The tone control only lowers each
hop further.

Spec surface: `Delay.hops`

## Digital

**`digital_mix_between_inputs`**: A crossfade of two 16-bit values stays between them (give or take one rounding step
toward zero), for any balance up to 65535.

Why it matters: the macro shapes and the wavetable engines mix with this; it cannot exceed its louder input, so it
cannot wrap.

Spec surface: `Digital.mix`

**`digital_clip_never_wraps`**: Braids' `CLIP` gives a value between -32767 and 32767, and the 16-bit sample written is
exactly that value.

Why it matters: the engines that end in a clip (saw swarm, harmonics, FOF vowel, question mark) are limited by the clip
only, never a wrap.

Spec surface: `Digital.clip`

**`digital_fm_never_wraps`**: Each of the three FM engines (FM, feedback FM, chaotic FM) writes exactly the blended sine
at its modulated phase; the cut to 16 bits changes nothing.

Why it matters: an FM sample is always a sine table value (`analog_sine_between_entries`), so no modulation depth,
feedback or sync can overflow it.

Spec surface: `Analog.sineAt`, `Digital.chaoticFm`, `Digital.feedbackFm`, `Digital.fm`, `attenuation`

**`digital_ring_product_never_wraps`**: The ring modulator's product of two 16-bit signals is their exact product
divided by 65536, between -16384 and 16384; the multiplication never wraps and the cut to 16 bits changes nothing.

Why it matters: the ring modulator multiplies three full-scale sines in a row, and the soft clipper after them gets a
value of at most half scale.

Spec surface: `Digital.ringProduct`

**`digital_ring_mod_never_wraps`**: The ring modulator writes exactly its soft-clipped triple product; the cut to 16
bits changes nothing.

Why it matters: the sample is always a value of the overdrive shaper, so it cannot overflow.

Spec surface: `Analog.shape`, `Analog.sineAt`, `Digital.overdriveTable`, `Digital.ringMod`, `Digital.ringProduct`,
`shape`

**`digital_toy_never_wraps`**: The toy's smoothing filter writes exactly the weighted average of its four held values,
centred on zero (between -28208 and 27987); the unsigned subtraction wraps for a low average and the read as a 16-bit
value undoes that wrap exactly.

Why it matters: the wrap Braids relies on is proved harmless; the sample is never off by 65536.

Spec surface: `Digital.toyFilter`

## DigitalSetup

**`digital_setup_fm_quantizer_in_table`**: A non-negative parameter reads two neighbouring entries of the 129-entry FM
ratio quantizer, both inside it. Braids' parameters are 0 to 32767, so `nonneg` holds for every block; a negative one
would read 65535 entries past the table.

Why it matters: the quantizer is C data read without a check, once per block for the three FM engines.

Spec surface: `DigitalSetup.fmQuantizerIndex`, `fmQuantizerIndex`

**`digital_setup_increments_half_cycle`**: Every phase step the setup hands to the FM, ring modulator, toy and digital
modulation sample code is below half a cycle per sample. The detuned pitches are cut to 16 bits before the lookup, so no
offset escapes the clamp.

Why it matters: a step at or above half a cycle would alias, and the modulators' detunes can reach pitches far outside
the carrier's range.

Spec surface: `DigitalSetup.fmModulatorIncrement`, `DigitalSetup.modulationSymbolIncrement`,
`DigitalSetup.ringIncrement`, `DigitalSetup.toyIncrement`, `fmModulatorIncrement`, `modulationSymbolIncrement`, `pitch`,
`ringIncrement`, `toyIncrement`

**`digital_setup_delay_in_table`**: Every pitch reads two neighbouring entries of the 97-entry delay table, both inside
it.

Why it matters: the table is C data read without a check; the clamp to note 128 and the fold into one octave keep the
read inside for any 16-bit pitch, including the lowest a filtered comb pitch reaches.

Spec surface: `DigitalSetup.delayIndex`, `delayIndex`, `pitch`

## Divider

**`divider_pulse_count`**: A divide-by-`div` counter started at 0 gives exactly `ticks / div` pulses after `ticks`
ticks.

Why it matters: the clock divider never drops or doubles a beat.

Spec surface: `Divider.run`

**`divider_count_bounded`**: The count stays below `div`.

Why it matters: the counter stays bounded however long it runs.

Spec surface: `Divider.run`, `below`

## Drum

**`drum_noise_period`**: The noise register from state 1 returns to 1 after exactly 65535 steps and not before: its
period is 65535, every nonzero 16-bit state (the taps are a primitive polynomial, checked by walking the orbit in the
kernel).

Why it matters: the noise behind the kick's click, the snare, the clap and the toms repeats only every 1.5 s, longer
than any hit.

Spec surface: `Drum.lfsrAfter`

**`drum_noise_never_dies`**: A nonzero 16-bit noise state steps to a nonzero 16-bit state, checked for every state.

Why it matters: the glue seeds the register with 1 and never resets it; a register at zero would stay there.

Spec surface: `Drum.lfsr`

**`drum_noise_in_range`**: The noise sample is within 32767 for any state.

Why it matters: the noise feeds the filters and the gains at the same scale as a sine, so the bounds below cover it.

Spec surface: `Drum.noise`

**`drum_one_pole_stays_between`**: A one-pole step is exact, `y + (x - y) * a / 16384` rounded down, and lies between
the state `y` and the input `x`, for a coefficient from 0 to 16384 and signals within 65535; so the high-pass (input
minus low-pass) is within `|x| + |y|`, and nothing wraps.

Why it matters: the low-pass cannot overshoot and the high-pass built from it is bounded, which is what lets six squares
and a noise pass through two filters without a 32-bit wrap.

Spec surface: `Drum.highPass`, `Drum.onePole`

**`drum_envelope_only_falls`**: The envelope one sample on is no higher, and no lower than 15/16 of itself less one
count, for any rate below 65536 and envelope up to one; rate 0 holds exactly; nothing wraps.

Why it matters: a drum's envelope only falls, by at most a sixteenth a sample, and rate 0 is a hold.

Spec surface: `Drum.envelope`

**`drum_sweep_lands`**: The swept increment never rises and never goes below its target: from above it falls by `rate /
2^20` of the distance a sample, at or below it stays on the target; for any increment below half a cycle a sample and
any rate below 8192, nothing wraps.

Why it matters: the kick's and toms' pitch sweeps land on the note with no undershoot, from a start up to five times
above it.

Spec surface: `Drum.sweep`

**`drum_sweep_start_below_nyquist`**: A sweep's start is below half a cycle a sample for any tuning and colour.

Why it matters: `drum_sweep_lands` needs the swept increment below Nyquist, and the start is its highest value; a note
above the 1.4 kHz hold starts on its tuning.

Spec surface: `Drum.sweepStart`

**`drum_metal_sum_fits`**: Six squares of ±5461 sum within 32766 for any six phases.

Why it matters: the hi-hats and the cymbal enter their filters in the same range as a sine or the noise, so the same
filter and gain bounds cover them.

Spec surface: `Drum.metal`

**`drum_square_in_range`**: A square is ±5461.

Why it matters: the cowbell's two squares sum within 10922, inside the bound the band filter needs.

Spec surface: `Drum.square`

**`drum_gain_never_amplifies`**: A gain is at most its level, for any envelope up to one and level up to 32767.

Why it matters: a shape's loudest moment is its level table, which `drum_output_never_wraps` relies on.

Spec surface: `Drum.gain`

**`drum_metal_chain_bounded`**: Six squares through the band filter and the gain: the sum is within 32766
(`drum_metal_sum_fits`), the first low-pass state within 32767, the high-pass within 65534, the second low-pass within
65535 and the gained output within 262140, for filter coefficients from 0 to 16384 (Q14) and a gain up to 32767 (Q13);
no stage wraps and the states handed on satisfy the same bounds.

Why it matters: the hi-hats and the cymbal are the most arithmetic of any shape, and no colour setting can overflow
their filters.

Spec surface: `Drum.bandOut`, `Drum.highPass`, `Drum.metal`, `Drum.onePole`

**`drum_output_never_wraps`**: Every shape's mix, as `voices/drum_glue.c` composes it, is exact and within 524280 (under
2^19), for filter states within their bounds (first stage within 32767, second within 65535), coefficients from 0 to
16384 and gains up to 32767: the kick and toms (`Drum.tunedOut`), the snare (`Drum.snareOut`), the clap
(`Drum.clapOut`), the hi-hats and cowbell (`Drum.bandOut`, with `drum_metal_chain_bounded` and `drum_square_in_range`)
and the cymbal (`Drum.cymbalOut`); the sample out is the mix clipped to 16 bits, unchanged when already within 32767.

Why it matters: no drum's arithmetic wraps at any knob, note or phase; an overloaded shape clips.

Spec surface: `Drum.bandOut`, `Drum.clapOut`, `Drum.cymbalOut`, `Drum.metal`, `Drum.saturate`, `Drum.snareOut`,
`Drum.square`, `Drum.tunedOut`, `cymbalOut`

**`drum_shapes_in_range`**: Every shape's tables are in the ranges the theorems above need: rates below 65536 at every
timbre (`drum_envelope_only_falls`), sweep rates below 8192 (`drum_sweep_lands`), levels up to 32767
(`drum_gain_never_amplifies`) and filter coefficients up to 16384 (`drum_one_pole_stays_between`) at every colour.

Why it matters: the table values are checked against the bounds, so a knob at either end still meets every hypothesis.

Spec surface: `Drum.decay`, `Drum.highCoefficient`, `Drum.level`, `Drum.lowCoefficient`, `Drum.rate`,
`Drum.secondLevel`, `Drum.secondRate`, `Drum.sweepRate`, `Drum.upperCoefficient`, `shape`

**`drum_tails`**: Each shape's tail from a full envelope at its nominal rate, stepped sample by sample in the kernel:
the first sample at which the envelope is under a hundredth, with the sample before still above. BD (rate 219) in 21984
samples (0.50 s), SD and the clap's tail (729) in 6617 (0.15 s), CH (2200) in 2193 (50 ms), OH (270) in 17842 (0.40 s),
LT (365) in 13206 (0.30 s), MT (437) in 11033 (0.25 s), HT and CB (547) in 8817 (0.20 s), CY (73) in 65568 (1.49 s).

Why it matters: these are the decay times a player hears, computed from the arithmetic the chip runs.

Spec surface: `Drum.envelopeAfter`, `Drum.rate`

## Euclid

**`euclid_hit_count`**: A bar of `steps` steps with `hits` hits (`hits ≤ steps`) has exactly `hits` hits.

Why it matters: the pattern has exactly the density HITS asks for.

Spec surface: `Euclid.countHits`

**`euclid_last_step_hits`**: The last step of a bar is a hit whenever `hits > 0`.

Why it matters: every Euclidean pattern ends on a hit; the ratchet's first sub-hit relies on this.

Spec surface: `Euclid.hit`

**`euclid_window_balanced`**: Any window of `len` steps, wherever it starts (wrapping across the bar line), contains
`len * hits / steps` hits or one more.

Why it matters: the hits never bunch; this is Clough–Douthett maximal evenness for the pattern the firmware plays.

Spec surface: `Euclid.countBetween`

**`euclid_windows_within_one`**: Two windows of the same length differ by at most one hit.

Why it matters: any two bars, half-bars or beats carry the same number of hits or one apart.

Spec surface: `Euclid.countBetween`

**`euclid_is_christoffel`**: For `hits < steps`, step `k` is a hit exactly when `(k + 1) * hits` wraps modulo `steps`:
the pattern is the lower Christoffel word of slope `hits / steps` in Berstel's form (`hits = steps` is every step).

Why it matters: known results on Christoffel words (balance, conjugates are the Euclidean rhythms, reversal is the upper
word) apply to these patterns.

Spec surface: `Euclid.christoffel`, `Euclid.hit`

**`euclid_bjorklund_is_rotation`**: Bjorklund's algorithm (Toussaint's form) gives a rotation of `Euclid.hit`, for every
bar of up to 32 steps and every hit count, checked case by case.

Why it matters: the firmware's floor-function pattern is the usual "Euclidean rhythm" up to rotation, which ROTATE
reaches.

Spec surface: `Euclid.bjorklund`, `Euclid.word`

**`euclid_major_scale_is_seven_of_twelve`**: The major scale mask is the 7-of-12 Euclidean pattern started on step 6;
the major pentatonic mask is the 5-of-12 pattern started on step 7.

Why it matters: the SCALE table and the Euclidean rhythm are one construction (Clough–Douthett).

Spec surface: `Euclid.mask`, `Harmony.scale`

## Filter

**`filter_open_is_identity`**: With the cutoff fully open (100) and no resonance the filter is inactive: a step returns
the sample and the states unchanged, for any state, mode and sample.

Why it matters: these are the default settings on every voice, so every existing sound is bit for bit what it was.

Spec surface: `Filter.active`, `Filter.step`

**`filter_states_stay_bounded`**: The states and nodes stay within ±65536 and nothing in a sample wraps: a state after a
sample is within ±65536 for any inputs (the hold); and with both states within ±65536, the sample within ±65536 and the
coefficients in range (`g`, `a1`, `a2` at most 32767, `k` at most 16384) the band node before its hold is exactly `a1
ic1 / 2^15 + a2 ((x - ic2) / 2) / 2^14`, the held band node `v1` is within ±65536, the low-pass node `v2` is exactly
`ic2 + g v1 / 2^15` and within ±131072, the high-pass output before its clip is exactly `x - k v1 / 2^13 - v2`, the raw
output of every mode is within ±327680 (5 · 2^16), and both state updates `2 v - ic` are exact.

Why it matters: the filter's arithmetic saturates at twice full scale and never wraps, whatever the engine feeds it; the
clip to ±32767 at the output is the only other thing that touches a sample.

Spec surface: `Filter.band`, `Filter.next`, `Filter.raw`, `Filter.v1`, `Filter.v2`

**`filter_coefficients_in_range`**: The coefficients are in the ranges `filter_states_stay_bounded` needs and the cutoff
sweep is monotone: every entry of the cutoff table is at most 32767 and the table rises from each setting to the next
below 100; the damping is between 884 (0.108 in Q13) and 16384 (2.0) for any resonance setting; `a1` is at most 32767
for any `g` and `k`, and `a2` is at most `g` for any `g` at most 32767.

Why it matters: turning the cutoff up always opens the filter, and no cutoff or resonance setting hands the sample path
a coefficient that could overflow a product.

Spec surface: `Filter.a1`, `Filter.a2`, `Filter.g`, `Filter.k`

**`filter_settled_dc_holds`**: A settled constant is a fixed point of the active filter: with the band state at rest
(`ic1 = 0`) and the low-pass state at the sample (`ic2 = x`), a step gives low-pass output `x`, band and high outputs 0,
and the same states again, for any cutoff and resonance and any sample within ±32767.

Why it matters: a steady level passes through the low-pass unchanged at any cutoff, with no drift from rounding, and the
band and high outputs reject it.

Spec surface: `Filter.active`, `Filter.step`, `cutoff`

**`filter_lowpass_steps_toward_input`**: A low-pass step with the band node at rest (`ic1 = 0`) lands between the
low-pass state and the sample, within one count of rounding below: `min x ic2 - 1 ≤ v2 ≤ max x ic2`, for any `g` and
`a2` at most 32767, any `a1`, and states and sample within ±65536.

Why it matters: the low-pass integrator only moves toward the input; a resonant peak comes from the band state, which is
held.

Spec surface: `Filter.v1`, `Filter.v2`

## Filters

**`filters_swarm_sum_range`**: The sum of the swarm's seven saws lies between -28672 and 28665 at any phases.

Why it matters: the sum is offset by 32768 and read from the overdrive shaper with no mask; this keeps the read inside
its 257 entries (`tables_shape_reads_inside_257`).

Spec surface: `Filters.swarmSum`

**`filters_harmonic_glide_approaches`**: A harmonic's amplitude moves toward its target and never passes it (shown for
an amplitude below its target; the other direction is symmetric), for values within a quarter of the 32-bit range.

Why it matters: a fading harmonic cannot overshoot.

Spec surface: `Filters.harmonicGlide`, `harmonicGlide`

**`filters_swarm_filter_never_wraps`**: The saw swarm's high-pass writes exactly its clipped output, between -32767 and
32767.

Why it matters: the swarm's sample is limited by the clip only; a resonance peak cannot wrap.

Spec surface: `Digital.clip`, `Filters.swarmFilter`, `damp`

**`filters_windowed_carrier_never_wraps`**: The windowed carrier of the digital filter engines is the exact product the
formula means, for a 16-bit carrier and a window of 0 to 65535. In the low-pass and peak types the product can pass 2^31

Why it matters: the digital filter engines never write a wrapped carrier; the overflow in the original is proved
harmless.

Spec surface: `Filters.windowedCarrier`

**`filters_vosim_never_wraps`**: Vosim writes exactly its windowed formant sum, centred on zero; the two formants under
the bell window never pass 49150 before the centring, so nothing wraps at any pitch or formant setting.

Why it matters: vosim cannot overflow for any formant frequencies.

Spec surface: `Analog.sineAt`, `Filters.bellAt`, `Filters.vosim`

**`filters_harmonics_never_wraps`**: The harmonics engine writes exactly its clipped sum, and the in-between sample
exactly the average of that with the last sample.

Why it matters: the sum is limited by the clip only and the half-rate smoothing cannot wrap.

Spec surface: `Digital.clip`, `Filters.harmonicsMid`, `Filters.harmonicsOut`, `harmonicsMid`, `harmonicsOut`

**`filters_setup_cutoff_in_table`**: The swarm's high-pass reads two neighbouring entries of the 257-entry cutoff table,

**`filters_setup_table_blend_le_top`**: A table of 16-bit entries that rise from one to the next across its 257 entries

## Fm

**`fm_sine_bounded`**: The sine is within ±32767 for every phase.

Why it matters: an operator never hands its modulators or the mix more than full scale; there is no table, and the
polynomial is checked for every input.

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_sine_exact_at_quarters`**: The sine is 0 at the start, 32767 at a quarter, 0 at a half and -32767 at three
quarters (32767 is one).

Why it matters: an operator at rest is zero and a peak is exactly full scale, so the shapes' levels and depths mean what
they say.

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_sine_continuous`**: From one position to the next the quarter sine rises by at most two and falls by at most one
(the fall is rounding), for every position.

Why it matters: the polynomial has no step, so no click or buzz of its own; checked at all 32767 positions.

Spec surface: `Fm.quarter`

**`fm_operator_never_wraps`**: An operator's output is its sine through its envelope, at most 32767 in size for any
envelope up to one (2^24), and nothing wraps.

Why it matters: an operator's output is in range whatever its modulators did, so no depth setting can overflow it.

Spec surface: `Fm.operator`, `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_carry_never_amplifies`**: A carrier's share is at most its output, for a level at most one (32767).

Why it matters: the mix of carriers is at most the sum of their levels, so a shape with levels summing to one cannot
clip (`fm_shapes_in_range`).

Spec surface: `Fm.carry`

**`fm_saturate_in_range`**: The voice's output sample is within ±32767, and a sum already in range passes unchanged.

Why it matters: the hold at full scale is a saturation, not a wrap.

Spec surface: `Fm.saturate`

**`fm_envelope_only_falls`**: The envelope one block on is no higher, and no lower than 15/16 of itself, for any rate
below 65536 and envelope up to one; nothing wraps.

Why it matters: an operator's envelope only falls, by at most a sixteenth a block.

Spec surface: `Fm.envelopeStep`

**`fm_increment_below_nyquist`**: An operator's increment is at most half a cycle a sample for any pitch and ratio.

Why it matters: an operator asked for more than Nyquist is held there instead of wrapping to a wrong pitch.

Spec surface: `Fm.increment`

**`fm_algorithms_well_founded`**: In every algorithm a modulator is a higher-numbered operator than the one it
modulates, and operator 1 always carries.

Why it matters: computing operators 4 down to 1, every modulator's output is already this sample's (no one-sample lag
and no cycle except the declared feedback on 4), and every algorithm makes a sound.

Spec surface: `Fm.carries`, `Fm.modulates`

**`fm_shapes_in_range`**: Every shape's settings are in the ranges the theorems above need: ratios at most 16 (4096 in
Q8), levels at most one (32767), rates below 65536, and a shape's carrier levels sum to at most one.

Why it matters: with `fm_carry_never_amplifies` the eight shapes cannot clip at full envelope, and
`fm_envelope_only_falls` applies to their rates.

Spec surface: `Fm.level`, `Fm.rate`, `Fm.ratio`, `shape`

## FmAlgorithms

**`fm_no_operator_idle`**: In every algorithm every operator either carries or modulates some operator; none of the four
is wired to nothing.

Why it matters: an operator that neither sounds nor modulates would be a quarter of the voice computed every sample for
nothing.

Spec surface: `Fm.carries`, `Fm.modulates`

**`fm_algorithms_distinct`**: The eight algorithms are pairwise different: any two differ in some modulation link or in
which operators carry.

Why it matters: no two positions of the algorithm knob wire the operators the same way.

Spec surface: `Fm.carries`, `Fm.modulates`

**`fm_every_shape_uses_four_operators`**: In every shape every operator is either a carrier of the shape's algorithm or
has nonzero depth and modulates some operator in it; no shape has a silent modulator.

Why it matters: a modulator with depth zero would run its sine and envelope every sample for nothing.

Spec surface: `Fm.algorithm`, `Fm.carries`, `Fm.depth`, `Fm.modulates`, `shape`

## FmDeviation

**`fm_sine_lipschitz`**: The sine is Lipschitz in the phase: between two phases it moves by at most two per position
(2^15 phase units) crossed, plus one more for the partial positions at either end.

Why it matters: a bounded phase step reads a bounded change in value, which is what bounds a modulator's slew and a
carrier's frequency deviation (`fm_modulator_slew`, `fm_carrier_frequency_deviation`).

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_modulator_slew`**: An unmodulated operator at increment `incM` and envelope `env` (at most one) changes by at most
`2 (incM / 2^15 + 1) + 1` a sample: two per position its phase crosses, one for the envelope rounding. The phase wraps
round the cycle as the engine's does; the sine is continuous across the wrap (both ends are zero), so the wrap costs
nothing.

Why it matters: a modulator's slew is proportional to its increment, which is what makes FM's frequency deviation
proportional to the modulating frequency.

Spec surface: `Fm.operator`, `Fm.phaseAfter`

**`fm_carrier_frequency_deviation`**: A carrier at increment `incC` pushed by a modulator (`Fm.push 0 out depth`, `out`
the modulator's output each sample): the push changes from one sample to the next by `depth` times the change in `out`
(modulo 2^32), so the pushed phase advances by `incC` plus that term; the change in `out` is at most `2 (incM / 2^15 +
1) + 1` (`fm_modulator_slew`), so the change in the push, as an integer, is at most `depth` times that.

Why it matters: the carrier's peak frequency deviation is at most `depth (2 (incM / 2^15 + 1) + 1)` phase units a
sample, proportional to modulation index times modulator frequency, as Carson's rule says.

Spec surface: `Fm.operator`, `Fm.phaseAfter`, `Fm.push`

## FmEnvelope

**`fm_envelope_after_bounded`**: The envelope after `k` blocks at `rate` is at most its start times `((2^20 - rate) /
2^20)^k` plus 17 counts a block for rounding (both sides scaled by `2^20^k` to stay in whole numbers), for any start up
to one (2^24) and rate below 65536; and it never passes one.

Why it matters: an envelope decays geometrically with time constant `2^20 / rate` blocks; rounding cannot pile up into a
floor.

Spec surface: `Fm.envelopeAfter`

**`fm_held_envelope_holds`**: At rate 0 the envelope holds exactly.

Why it matters: `0` in the rate table means hold with no drift from rounding (the piano's body, every organ operator).

Spec surface: `Fm.envelopeAfter`

**`fm_shape_tails`**: For every shape's decaying operators, from a full envelope (2^24) at the table's rate: the first
block at which the envelope is under a tenth, and under a hundredth, stepped block by block in the kernel, with the
block before still above (a block is 16 samples, 0.36 ms at 44.1 kHz). Under a hundredth: the piano's tine (op 4) in
0.27 s, its modulator (op 2) in 1.8 s, its op 3 in 3.7 s; the bell's three partials in 1.8 to 4.4 s; the bass's
modulators in 0.4 to 0.9 s; the brass's in 7 to 14 s; the glass's op 4 in 5.5 s; the mallet's in 0.16 to 0.44 s; the
clang's in 1.2 to 2.2 s. The organ and every shape's carriers hold (`fm_held_envelope_holds`).

Why it matters: these are the decay times a player hears, computed from the arithmetic the chip runs.

Spec surface: `Fm.envelopeAfter`, `Fm.rate`

## FmLock

**`fm_harmonic_ratios_lock`**: With both starting at phase 0, an operator at integer ratio `r` (Q8 value `r * 256`) has
phase exactly `r` times the carrier's after any number of samples, modulo 2^32.

Why it matters: an integer ratio is exactly harmonic; the two phases never drift apart or beat.

Spec surface: `Fm.increment`, `Fm.phaseAfter`

**`fm_integer_ratio_periodic`**: The operator's waveform at integer ratio `r` is a function of the carrier's phase
alone.

Why it matters: carrier plus operator is then periodic with the carrier, so the spectrum is exactly harmonic.

Spec surface: `Fm.increment`, `Fm.phaseAfter`, `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_organ_and_bass_lock`**: FM ORGAN (shape 54) and FM BASS (shape 50) use integer ratios on every operator; FM BELL
(shape 49) does not.

Why it matters: the organ and bass are exactly harmonic by `fm_harmonic_ratios_lock`; the bell's ratios of 3.5, 7.2 and
1.4 are inharmonic by design.

Spec surface: `Fm.ratio`

## FmRange

**`fm_cap_is_a_hold`**: The cap is a saturation of the exact value: the increment is the product shifted by eight, or
half a cycle, whichever is smaller.

Why it matters: above the keyboard an operator holds at Nyquist instead of wrapping to a wrong pitch; below it nothing
is lost.

Spec surface: `Fm.increment`

**`fm_increment_exact_below_cap`**: Below the cap the increment is the exact product shifted by eight: a product below
2^39 shifts to below 2^31, so neither the hold nor the narrowing to 32 bits changes it.

Why it matters: an operator's pitch is exactly the voice's times the shape's ratio, with no rounding beyond the Q8
shift.

Spec surface: `Fm.increment`

**`fm_no_cap_on_the_keyboard`**: For MIDI notes 0 to 88 (up to E6, 1319 Hz) every operator's increment is the exact
product of the voice's increment and its shape's ratio, shifted by eight; the Nyquist cap never engages. The voice's
increment is `Pitch.phaseIncrement` at `note * 128` (1/128 semitones), below 2^27 there, and every ratio is at most 16.0
(`fm_shapes_in_range`), so the product is below 2^39.

Why it matters: every FM shape's ratios are exact across the playable keyboard; above it the engine saturates at Nyquist
(`fm_cap_is_a_hold`).

Spec surface: `Fm.increment`, `Fm.ratio`, `Pitch.phaseIncrement`, `phaseIncrement`, `shape`

## FmSymmetry

**`fm_sine_half_wave`**: The sine half a cycle on is the negative of the sine: `sineAt (phase + 2^31) = -sineAt phase`
for every phase (the quadrant moves by two, the place in the quarter is unchanged, the sign flips).

Why it matters: a half-wave-symmetric waveform has no DC and no even harmonics, so the polynomial's error sits at odd
harmonics only.

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_sine_quarter_wave`**: The sine is mirrored about the quarter points: `sineAt (0x7FFFFFFF - phase) = sineAt phase`
for every phase. Position `p` (the phase's top 17 bits) pairs with position `65535 - p` (modulo 2^17), so place `x` in
an even quadrant pairs with place `32767 - x` in the odd quadrant above it, which `Fm.folded` reads at the same `x`.
(`0x80000000 - phase` would land one position off: the mirror of position 0 is 65535, not 65536.)

Why it matters: the engine evaluates the polynomial on 0 to 32767 only and reads the other three quarters by mirror and
sign; this symmetry is what makes that exact.

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_sine_pairs_cancel`**: Two samples half a cycle apart cancel exactly: `sineAt p + sineAt (p + 2^31) = 0` as
integers for every phase (the negation cannot wrap, the sine being within 32767).

Why it matters: this is the pairing behind `fm_sine_has_no_dc`.

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

**`fm_sine_has_no_dc`**: Any 256 samples of the sine spaced a 256th of a cycle apart sum to zero, from any starting
phase: sample `k` and sample `k + 128` are half a cycle apart and cancel (`fm_sine_half_wave`).

Why it matters: a carrier at any pitch and phase adds no DC to the mix over a cycle, and the polynomial's error has no
even harmonics.

Spec surface: `Fm.sineAt`

## Harmony

**`harmony_mask_never_empty`**: The mask handed to a lane is non-empty and fits in 12 bits, for every scale, key,
progression, chord step and follow mode, Tonnetz walks included.

Why it matters: `lane_note_in_scale` needs a non-empty 12-bit mask; a chord change can never leave a lane with nothing
to play.

Spec surface: `harmonyMask`

**`harmony_follow_in_key`**: On a table progression with chord follow 7TH or TRIAD, every note a lane may play is in the
key's scale: the chord is stacked from the key's scale at the chord root, not a transposed shape. (The ii chord of C
major is D F A, not D F# A.) Tonnetz walks are excluded on purpose; see `harmony_walk_chord_is_triad`.

Why it matters: chord follow on a table progression stays in key.

Spec surface: `harmonyMask`

**`harmony_progression_chord_has_root`**: With chord follow 7TH or TRIAD the chord's root is among the lane's notes, for
every scale and key, on tables and walks alike.

Why it matters: a following lane can play the root, not only upper notes of the chord.

Spec surface: `harmonyChordRoot`, `harmonyMask`

**`harmony_chord_root_in_key`**: On a table progression every chord root is a note of the key's scale (below an octave).

Why it matters: progressions are written as degrees, so 1-4-5-1 is I IV V in major and i iv v in minor; a table chord
never lands outside the key. A walk's root can.

Spec surface: `Harmony.scale`, `harmonyChordRoot`

**`harmony_walk_chord_is_triad`**: On a Tonnetz walk (progressions 8 and up) with chord follow 7TH or TRIAD, a lane
plays exactly the walk's triad transposed to the key: three notes, the chord the screen names.

Why it matters: walks leave the key on purpose, and the lane plays exactly the named triad.

Spec surface: `Harmony.rotate`, `Harmony.size`, `Harmony.walkChord`, `Tonnetz.mask`, `harmonyMask`

**`harmony_walk_keeps_two_notes`**: Consecutive chords of any Tonnetz walk share exactly two pitch classes.

Why it matters: P, L and R each change one note of a triad, so a walk moves one note at a time.

Spec surface: `Harmony.size`, `Tonnetz.mask`, `Tonnetz.walk`

**`harmony_complement_outside_key`**: COMPL (chord follow 3) plays exactly the notes outside the key's scale, for every
scale, key and chord; the chromatic scale plays whole.

Why it matters: a COMPL lane never shares a note with a lane in the key.

Spec surface: `harmonyMask`

**`harmony_complement_interval_content`**: The complement theorem (Lewin's generalisation of Babbitt's hexachord
theorem) for any cycle `Z_n` and any set: the ordered count of pairs at distance `d` in the complement equals the count
in the set plus `n - 2 * size`. For `size = n / 2` the two are equal.

Why it matters: a scale and the notes it leaves out share their interval content, which is why COMPL sounds like the key
it was cut from.

Spec surface: `Harmony.count`, `Harmony.pairs`

**`harmony_hexachord_theorem`**: Babbitt's hexachord theorem on the 12-bit masks: every six-note mask has the interval
vector of its complement, checked for all 4096 masks.

Why it matters: the `n = 12`, `size = 6` instance of `harmony_complement_interval_content` on the masks the lanes play
from.

Spec surface: `Harmony.intervalVector`, `Harmony.size`

## Reverb

**`reverb_position_in_ring`**: A sample position is inside the shared delay memory for any write position, line start
and offset.

Why it matters: no knob setting can make a read or write land outside the memory.

Spec surface: `Reverb.pos`

**`reverb_lines_fit_and_do_not_overlap`**: At every scale up to 400 (353 shipped), each delay line ends inside the
memory and each line starts one sample after the previous one ends.

Why it matters: the ten lines never overlap and none runs off the end.

Spec surface: `Reverb.length`, `Reverb.start`

**`reverb_gains_below_one`**: For every decay-time setting the loop gain is positive and at most 0.98, and for every
brightness setting the low-pass amount is positive and at most 0.97.

Why it matters: no knob position gives a gain of 1.0 or more. This is about the settings only, not stability of the
whole network.

Spec surface: `Reverb.brightGain`, `Reverb.timeGain`

**`reverb_compress_saturates`**: The value stored in the delay memory is the signal divided by 8, rounded towards zero,
saturated at the 16-bit ends: from 262144 up it stores 32767, from -262152 down it stores -32768, and in between it is
within 7 of the signal (times 8).

Why it matters: a loud signal in the loops clips instead of wrapping to the opposite sign.

Spec surface: `Reverb.compress`

**`reverb_gain_never_amplifies`**: A signal times a gain between 0 and 1.0 lies between zero and the signal. (The most
negative 32-bit number is excluded; nothing in the reverb comes near it.)

Why it matters: the loop gain and the low-pass amount are such gains, so a pass round a loop cannot make a sample
louder, rounding included.

Spec surface: `Reverb.mul`

**`reverb_lowpass_stays_between`**: One step of the low-pass lands between its old state and the new signal, for any
amount from 0 to 1.0, when both are within a quarter of the 32-bit range (the loops hold at most 8.0).

Why it matters: the filter in each loop cannot overshoot or add level.

Spec surface: `Reverb.lowpass`

**`reverb_interpolation_stays_between`**: A read between two stored neighbours lies between them, for any fraction below
1.0, when both are within a quarter of the 32-bit range.

Why it matters: the interpolation of a modulated delay cannot add a spike.

Spec surface: `Reverb.interpolate`

**`reverb_lfo_in_range`**: The LFO's wobble is between 0 and 32767 (0 and 1.0 in Q15) at every phase.

Why it matters: a modulated delay stays between its base time and its base plus its depth.

Spec surface: `Reverb.lfo`

**`reverb_modulated_read_stays_in_line`**: The two modulated reads, the first diffuser (10 samples plus up to 60, line 0
of 113) and the long delay of the second loop (4680 plus up to 100, line 9 of 4782), read a sample and its older
neighbour that are both inside their own line, for any LFO value, at every scale from 192 to 400 (353 shipped; below 192
the long delay's two-sample margin is lost to rounding).

Why it matters: a modulated read past the end of its line would read the next line's samples, which the differential
test could miss.

Spec surface: `Reverb.length`, `Reverb.modOffset`

**`reverb_round_trip_decays`**: One trip round the undriven tank (through both loops) scales a sample by the decay gain
squared plus at most one unit of rounding: in Q15, a sample read from a long delay comes back to it as at most `tail ·
decay² / 2^30 + 1`. With the gain at most 0.98 the squared gain is at most 0.96. This follows one sample's direct path
with the rest of the tank quiet; what the low-passes and allpasses hold comes out on later samples under the same gains.

Why it matters: with nothing fed in, every trip makes the tail quieter, at any decay setting.

Spec surface: `Reverb.decompress`, `Reverb.fromMemory`, `Reverb.roundTrip`

**`reverb_wet_never_overflows`**: The wet sample a loop hands to the mixer is exactly twice the sum the loop stores (no
wrap in the doubling or in any addition before it), at most 16448.0 in size (2^29 + 2^21), the low-pass's new state
stays within 8192.0 (2^28), and the mixer's return gain of up to 2.0 (`Mix.wetSample`) scales it with no wrap. Holds for
a send of at most 4096.0 (2^27), a low-pass state of at most 8192.0 (kept by the third claim from one sample to the
next, starting cleared), gains of at most 1.0 in size, and memory samples of at most 8.0.

Why it matters: the reverb's output cannot wrap to the opposite sign on its way into the mix.

Spec surface: `Mix.wetSample`, `Reverb.diffuse`, `Reverb.fromMemory`, `Reverb.loop`, `Reverb.loopSmooth`, `Reverb.mul`

**`reverb_tail_stays_in_memory`**: A sample read from the memory after any number of trips round an undriven tank is a
memory sample, at most 8.0 in size.

Why it matters: the 16-bit memory format caps a tail at any number of trips and any gains.

Spec surface: `Reverb.fromMemory`, `Reverb.trips`

**`reverb_tail_dies_away`**: After `n` trips round an undriven tank, a sample is at most its starting size times the
decay gain to the power `2n` (Q15, so divided by `2^30` per trip), plus 16385 (half a unit in Q15). The decay gain must
be at most 32767. The constant is needed: each trip rounds by up to one unit (`reverb_round_trip_decays`), and the gain
keeps the sum of those roundings below 16385. The memory stores steps of 8, so the tail settles within a few steps of
silence.

Why it matters: a tail at any level decays towards silence; rounding cannot accumulate into noise or a tone that never
ends.

Spec surface: `Reverb.fromMemory`, `Reverb.trips`

**`smooth_parameter_between`**: A smoothed parameter lies between `start` and `target`, for parameters 0 to 32767

## Tonnetz

**`tonnetz_moves_are_involutions`**: Each of P, L and R is an involution.

Spec surface: `Tonnetz.Triad`, `Tonnetz.move`

**`tonnetz_group_has_order_24`**: The group generated by P, L and R has exactly 24 elements: every word acts as one of

Spec surface: `Tonnetz.act`, `Tonnetz.apply`

**`tonnetz_simply_transitive`**: The group acts simply transitively on the triads: from any triad exactly one element

Why it matters: every triad is reachable from every other, and a word's effect is fixed by where it sends one triad.

Spec surface: `Tonnetz.Triad`, `Tonnetz.act`

**`tonnetz_commutes_with_transposition_and_inversion`**: The PLR group commutes with every transposition and every

**`tonnetz_group_is_dihedral`**: The dihedral relations: L R has order exactly 12, L has order 2, and L conjugates L R

Spec surface: `Tonnetz.apply`

**`tonnetz_walks_are_cycles`**: From any start, the L R walk visits all 24 triads once and returns after 24 moves; the P

Why it matters: L R is a Hamiltonian cycle on the Tonnetz; P L and P R are the two short cycles.

Spec surface: `Tonnetz.walk`

## Vowels

**`vowels_fof_contribution_never_wraps`**: Each formant filter of the FOF vowel contributes exactly its band-pass state
times the amplitude (a 16-bit value) divided by 131072, between -8192 and 8191, with nothing wrapping.

Why it matters: the five contributions are summed in 32 bits in C and never pass 40960, so the clip after them is the
only limit.

Spec surface: `Vowels.fofFilter`

**`vowels_fof_out_never_wraps`**: The FOF vowel writes exactly its clipped filter sum, and the in-between sample exactly
the average of that with the last sample.

Why it matters: the sum is limited by the clip only and the half-rate smoothing cannot wrap.

Spec surface: `Digital.clip`, `Vowels.fofOut`

**`vowels_question_drive_never_wraps`**: The question mark's distortion term is exactly the square-law formula, between
0 and 65535, for a clipped sample and a drive of 0 to 32767; neither product wraps.

Why it matters: full drive cannot overflow and flip the sample's sign; the clip after it is the only limit.

Spec surface: `Vowels.questionDrive`, `parameter`

## VowelsSetup

**`vowels_setup_vowel_index_in_table`**: A parameter of 0 to 32767 selects a vowel from 0 to 7, and the vowel after it
is at most 8, so both reads are inside the nine entries of `vowels_data`.

Why it matters: the vowel blends two neighbouring rows without a bounds check; the top three bits of the parameter never
name the last row, so the row after exists.

Spec surface: `VowelsSetup.vowelIndex`, `parameter`, `vowelIndex`

**`vowels_setup_consonant_index_in_table`**: A strike's random sample selects a consonant from 0 to 7, inside the eight
entries of `consonant_data`.

Why it matters: the consonant is read without a bounds check from the random generator; the mask to three bits keeps it
inside.

Spec surface: `VowelsSetup.consonantIndex`, `consonantIndex`

**`vowels_setup_fof_reads_in_table`**: A parameter of 0 to 32767 selects a row of the FOF vowel's 5-by-5 grid from 0 to
3, and the row after it is at most 4, so the four corners the blend reads are inside the grid.

Why it matters: `fofBlend` reads the corner past the selected row and column; the top two bits of a parameter never
select the last row, so that corner exists.

Spec surface: `VowelsSetup.fofRow`, `fofRow`, `parameter`

**`vowels_setup_formant_amplitude_in_range`**: The amplitude the vowel hands each formant is at most 15. Every amplitude
in `vowels_data` is at most 15 (`braids_data_vowel_amplitudes_are_nibbles`, checked against `digital_oscillator.cc`).

Why it matters: the formant tables are indexed by the phase's top nibble or-ed with the amplitude, and
`tables_formant_reads_inside_256` needs the amplitude to be a nibble.

Spec surface: `VowelsSetup.vowelAmplitude`, `parameter`, `vowelAmplitude`

**`vowels_setup_fof_amplitude_16bit`**: The amplitude the FOF vowel hands its formant filters is a signed 16-bit value,
for any parameters.

Why it matters: `vowels_fof_contribution_never_wraps` takes a 16-bit amplitude; the blend's final cut to 16 bits (the
original's `int16_t` return) makes that so for every block.

Spec surface: `VowelsSetup.fofAmplitude`, `fofAmplitude`

## Wavetables

**`wavetables_wavetable_never_wraps`**: A wavetable sample is exactly the sum of its two half-rate readings, each
halved; nothing wraps.

Why it matters: the wavetable engine cannot overflow for any waves, phase or morph position.

Spec surface: `Wavetables.crossfade`, `Wavetables.wavetable`

**`wavetables_crossfade_between_waves`**: A crossfade of two waves at the same phase lies between the two waves' values
there, for any two waves.

Why it matters: morphing cannot exceed the louder wave, even where the waves are far apart and Braids' C++ product wraps
(`blend_sixteen_between`).

Spec surface: `Wavetables.crossfade`, `Wavetables.read`

**`wavetables_read_between_bytes`**: A wave read lies between the two bytes it blends (each scaled to 16 bits), for any
wave and phase. The product inside wraps in 32 bits for steep waves (Braids' `wt_waves` has byte steps up to 253; the
C++ does the same), and the cut to 16 bits cancels the wrap exactly.

Why it matters: a wave stays within its bytes' range; the overflow in the original is proved harmless.

Spec surface: `Analog.tableIndex`, `Wavetables.read`, `Wavetables.waveByte`, `tableIndex`

**`wavetables_wave_map_never_wraps`**: A wave map sample is exactly the sum of its two half-rate readings, each already
halved; nothing wraps.

Why it matters: the wave map engine cannot overflow for any square, phase or position in the square.

Spec surface: `Wavetables.mapRead`, `Wavetables.waveMap`

**`wavetables_wave_line_never_wraps`**: A wave line sample is exactly the average of its two half steps; nothing wraps.

Why it matters: the wave line engine cannot overflow for any waves, masks, crossfades or balance.

Spec surface: `Wavetables.linePair`, `Wavetables.waveLine`

**`wavetables_paraphonic_never_wraps`**: A paraphonic sample is exactly the sum of its four voices divided by four;
nothing wraps.

Why it matters: four full-scale voices added in 32 bits stay far inside the range at any chord, phase or morph position.

Spec surface: `Wavetables.crossfade`, `Wavetables.paraphonic`

## WavetablesSetup

**`wavetables_setup_table_in_table`**: The wavetable engine reads its wavetable's definition inside the table of 20
definitions, and the pointer's step and the one after it inside the 17 steps of a definition, for a held parameter from
0 to 32767. Every definition in `digital_oscillator.cc` has 4, 8 or 16 steps (`braids_data_wavetable_steps_at_most_16`).

Why it matters: both are C arrays read without a check; the 17th entry of each definition is there for the read past the
last step.

Spec surface: `WavetablesSetup.tableIndex`, `WavetablesSetup.tableStep`, `tableIndex`, `tableStep`

**`wavetables_setup_map_in_table`**: The wave map reads its four corners inside the 256-entry map for parameters from 0
to 32767 and corners 0 or 1 away from the square's origin.

Why it matters: the map is a C array read without a check; the scaling to 15 squares keeps the corner after the last
column or row inside the 16 by 16 grid.

Spec surface: `WavetablesSetup.mapIndex`, `mapIndex`

**`wavetables_setup_line_in_table`**: The wave line reads the held parameter's wave and the scan's wave inside its
64-entry line, and the wave after the scan's at most one past it, for parameters from 0 to 32767 and a smoothed
parameter from 0 to 65534; both parameters stay in those ranges for the next block. The read one past the line is
Braids' own: at the top of the parameter's range the scan reaches the last wave and asks for the one after it. The port
keeps that read so the output matches Braids.

Why it matters: the line is a C array read without a check; the ranges are the invariant that bounds every block's
reads, starting from 0.

Spec surface: `WavetablesSetup.lineHeld`, `WavetablesSetup.lineScan`, `WavetablesSetup.smoothed`, `held`, `lineHeld`,
`lineScan`, `smoothed`

**`wavetables_setup_paraphonic_in_table`**: The paraphonic engine reads its chord and the one after it inside the 17
chords, and the first parameter's wave and the one after it inside the 33-entry short line, for parameters from 0 to
32767.

Why it matters: both are C arrays read without a check; the 17th chord and 33rd wave are there for the read past the
last entry.

Spec surface: `WavetablesSetup.chordIndex`, `chordIndex`

**`wavetables_setup_wave_in_bank`**: A wave numbered up to 255 reads its two bytes at any halved phase inside the
33024-byte bank of 129-byte waves (`wt_waves`, `WT_WAVES_SIZE` in `resources.h`). The selection tables above hold
numbers up to 255.

Why it matters: the bank is read without a check through `Wavetables.waveByte`, which adds the wave's offset to the
byte's index; 256 waves of 129 bytes is exactly the bank.

Spec surface: `Analog.tableIndex`, `tableIndex`

**`wavetables_setup_increments_half_cycle`**: Every detuned voice of the paraphonic engine steps below half a cycle per
sample (`pitch_increment_bounded`).

Why it matters: the detune is added to the pitch before the table's clamp, so none of the four voices runs past Nyquist.

Spec surface: `WavetablesSetup.voiceIncrement`, `pitch`, `voiceIncrement`
